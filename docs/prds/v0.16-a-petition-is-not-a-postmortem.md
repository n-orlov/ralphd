# v0.16 — a petition is not a postmortem

## Goal

v0.15 shipped the review-economics engine (#110/#111/#112) and its code is verified — approach
3's review independently re-ran all six lanes green at `a900357`. The wave itself still died:
four approaches, 199 iterations, ~$182.71, aborted by the operator with only record paperwork
left. The postmortem is unambiguous about why. The petition machinery adjudicates **at
approach-end review only**, so a run that correctly detects an impossible criterion mid-approach
(the v0.15 worker logged "criteria cannot be satisfied as written" for three tasks, iterations
~117–131) has no move except to burn its validation attempts, strand the task as `failed`, and
hand review a rejection it must issue — the petition gets filed by the *next* approach, one
rejection too late. And the stranding criteria themselves came from two generators: the planner
writing **self-referential or temporally impossible clauses** (verify with `HEAD~1`; "single
commit" on a deliverable whose own contract mandates a second one; "cite the commit that cures
finding 2" when that cure is two tasks in the future; a write-time occurrence count of a sha
that the task's own mandated additions increment), and the validator applying **hyper-literal
demands to untracked scratch state** (approach 4's task 001 landed its one-line fix, pushed,
tests green, notification recorded — and exhausted because `/run/ralphd/notes.md` *pointed to*
the artifact holding 19 stale-sha pairs instead of inlining them).

This wave (#114) moves the adjudication to the moment of detection and takes the pen away from
both generators, while keeping the judge exactly as strict as it is:

- **A (#114)** — **petition at the moment of impossibility**: a worker that declares an
  obligation or criterion unachievable files the petition immediately, and the engine runs a
  bounded mid-run adjudication episode with the review-hat model *before the next validation
  attempt*, instead of waiting for approach-end review.
- **B (#114)** — **a CONFIRMED petition may re-scope the task**: the adjudicating judge — never
  the run — may author a replacement for the affected task's `successCriteria`, recorded,
  re-fingerprinted, and disclosed. The PRD itself stays immutable by everyone inside the
  container.
- **C (#114)** — **exhaustion is a petition, not a failure**: a task that exhausts its
  validation ladder routes to a new `petitioned` status carrying its last-attempt handoff as
  the petition body, for adjudication — not to a `failed` status that guarantees the
  approach's rejection.
- **D (#114)** — **a plan-time lint for stranding criteria**: the five patterns catalogued
  above become plan-ingestion lints, so the criteria that stranded v0.15's tasks are rejected
  before a worker ever sees them.
- **E** — version 0.16.0, v0.15's row settled, the roadmap, and the inherited v0.15 paperwork
  (an entry-53 correction, the v0.15 closing addendum and late-cured terminal summary, the
  sweep-chain repair).
- **F** — the closure of #114 with evidence, the full suite, the self-discharging final
  record, the closing report, the terminal summary.

## Read this before anything else

1. **Your engine is 0.15.0 — the first run where A/B/C of v0.15 are live.** Structured
   findings with `severity` and `curable`, verdict from blocking findings only, the
   `rejected_incurable` pause-and-escalate edge, and engine-side petition surfacing and
   run-scope adjudication persistence all protect *you*. Use them: file petitions through the
   documented format (`docs/review-economics.md`) and trust the pause edge rather than
   replanning into a wall. What you *build* this wave (mid-run adjudication, criteria
   amendment, `petitioned`, the lint) is for future runs; do not claim your own run exercised
   it — tests are the whole of the evidence.
2. **The judge stays strict — that is a design constraint.** Review has been factually right in
   every rejection across five waves. Nothing here suppresses a finding or weakens a check.
   Mid-run adjudication moves *when* the judge speaks, not *how strictly*. The authority
   boundary of v0.15's Non-negotiable 3 extends: the run never grants itself a severity, a
   curability, a petition outcome, **or a criteria amendment**.
3. **The operator may be asleep for your entire run.** Petition rather than wait for an amend.
   Every time-anchored duty is governed by the late-cure rule.

## The contract — rubric, petitions, late cure, and this wave's additions

The materiality rubric, petition protocol and late-cure rule of `docs/review-economics.md`
apply to this contract in full (this PRD is that document's second instance by reference).
Petitions go in `artifacts/reports/v0.16-petitions.md`. Three additional rules, learned at
v0.15's expense, bind both the planner and the validator of this run:

1. **The scratch-state rule.** No success criterion, validation demand, or review finding may
   condition on the content or format of untracked run state — `/run/ralphd/notes.md`,
   handoff notes, or any file outside the repository tree — beyond the existence of an
   artifact a criterion explicitly declares. A pointer to an artifact file is sufficient
   evidence of its content; demanding the content be duplicated into scratch state is void.
2. **Sha-scoped figures.** Every count, tally, or numeric claim written into a tracked record
   names the commit it was computed at ("at `<sha>`: N"). A sha-scoped figure is never stale;
   an unscoped floating figure is a defect in the criterion that demanded it, not in the
   record that carries it.
3. **The stranding taxonomy is banned from this wave's own plan.** No task criterion may:
   anchor verification to a relational sha (`HEAD~1`, `HEAD^`, "this commit's parent");
   demand a single commit for a deliverable whose own criteria also mandate a separate
   disclosure, notification, or record commit; require citing the commit of a task ordered
   later in the same plan; require an unscoped write-time count of a string the task's own
   mandated output contains; or condition on scratch-state format (rule 1). If a criterion
   needs one of these, write the sha-scoped or order-honest form instead.

## What counts as done here

The disclosed-residual policy continues: documentation gaps, stale-but-labelled snapshots,
imprecision, and cosmetic defects are disclosed in the closing report's residual list, not
repaired. Not covered: the six requirements, a red test, a blocking-false claim, an issue
closed without evidence. One honest attempt per task; deviations stated, never silent.

## Sequencing rule

No task's success criteria may require a state that a later task creates. Land enabling code
before anything that must be green because of it. If you find such a dependency mid-wave,
split the criterion honestly rather than deviating silently.

## Planning rules

Flat plan. No task names another in `dependsOn` unless it genuinely cannot start without that
task's output. Never let a docs, report, closure or notification task depend on a code task.
The closing report depends on nothing; it states what it observes.

# The requirements

## A — petition at the moment of impossibility (#114)

Today a worker that concludes an obligation is unachievable can only log prose and keep
burning attempts; petitions are read from the workspace by `open_petitions_in_workspace()`
(`src/ralphd/engine/petitions.py:190`) and surfaced only to approach-end review via
`_petitions_review_context()` (`src/ralphd/engine/loop.py:6267`). Build:

1. **A structured impossibility declaration in the worker contract**: the worker names the
   obligation (requirement letter, DoD item, or its own task's criterion clause), the petition
   type (`environmental | temporal | contradictory-clauses | external | self-referential`),
   and the evidence. The engine writes/validates the petition entry itself (same format as
   `docs/review-economics.md`; once per obligation still holds — a duplicate is refused, not
   filed).
2. **A bounded mid-run adjudication episode**: on a filed declaration, the engine schedules an
   adjudication iteration with the **review-hat model** scoped to that petition (and any other
   open ones), before the task's next validation attempt. Its verdict uses the existing
   adjudication vocabulary: CONFIRMED converts the obligation to a disclosed residual for the
   rest of the run via the existing run-scope record (`record_confirmed_adjudications()`,
   `petitions.py:677`, surviving `_archive_approach` exactly as today); REFUTED states
   concretely what would satisfy the obligation and the task continues its ladder.
3. Approach-end review still sees every petition and adjudication (the existing context
   surface), and may still adjudicate anything left open. Nothing about its strictness or its
   independent checks changes.

## B — a CONFIRMED petition may re-scope the task (#114)

1. When a mid-run adjudication CONFIRMS a petition whose subject is the task's own
   `successCriteria` (types `contradictory-clauses` or `self-referential`), the adjudicating
   judge may emit a **replacement criteria text** for that task alone. The engine applies it:
   a `task.criteria_amended` event recording old and new text and the adjudication that
   authorized it, `criteriaFingerprint` recomputed (`_criteria_fingerprint`,
   `loop.py:6077`), the validation-attempt counter reset for the re-scoped criteria.
2. **Authority boundary, pinned by tests**: a worker- or planner-authored criteria edit is
   refused and is a blocking finding (the tamper-evidence pattern of v0.15's C.3); the judge
   cannot amend the PRD, the DoD, another task, or anything under `/config`; the amendment and
   its authorizing adjudication must be disclosed in the closing report.
3. A CONFIRMED petition about a **PRD obligation** (not a task criterion) converts it to a
   disclosed residual exactly as in v0.15 — no text is rewritten anywhere.

## C — exhaustion is a petition, not a failure (#114)

1. Today `TASK_FAILURE_VALIDATION_EXHAUSTED` (`src/ralphd/engine/state.py:2256`) lands the
   task in `failed` after `tierLadderExhausted` (`state.py:2364`, signal at `loop.py:6621`),
   which guarantees the next review rejects the approach. Instead: route the exhausted task to
   a new **`petitioned`** status, carrying its last-attempt handoff (already written into
   `validationNotes`) as the petition body — v0.15 approach 4's task 001 handoff was a
   complete, well-argued petition that had nowhere to go.
2. A `petitioned` task is adjudicated — by A's mid-run episode if available, otherwise by
   approach-end review. CONFIRMED: the task counts as accepted-with-residual; the
   `plan_complete` contract check accepts it (the v0.15 run died once on exactly that check
   refusing waived-but-`failed` tasks). REFUTED: the task becomes `failed` as today, with the
   refutation's "what would satisfy it" recorded.
3. Backward compatibility: a run whose contract has no petition protocol keeps today's
   `failed` routing — pinned by test, same default-safe pattern as v0.15's A.3.

## D — a plan-time lint for stranding criteria (#114)

1. Extend `lint_proposal()` (`src/ralphd/engine/plan_ingestion.py:177`) with lints for the
   five patterns in this PRD's contract rule 3 (relational sha anchors; single-commit demands
   on multi-part deliverables; forward citation of later tasks' commits; unscoped self-growing
   counts; scratch-state format conditions).
2. **Heed the #86 lesson** (`lint_own_terminal_state`, `plan_ingestion.py:313`, and its
   `_OWN_TERMINAL_STATE_CUES` false positive): scope cues to the clause, not the whole string;
   every lint lands with fixtures in both directions — the v0.15 criteria that stranded
   (approach 3 task 004's `HEAD~1` clause; approach 4 task 001's notes.md demand) as
   must-catch, and legitimate criteria that merely *mention* shas, commits, or counts as
   must-pass.
3. The lint refuses through the existing plan re-ask ladder, naming the offending clause and
   the sha-scoped or order-honest form that would pass.
