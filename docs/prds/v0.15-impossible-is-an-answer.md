# v0.15 — impossible is an answer

## Goal

Review has rejected eight consecutive approaches across three waves, and every finding was
factually correct. The judge is not the defect. The defect is that the loop cannot *accept an
answer other than "done"*: a verdict is binary over an unbounded finding surface, a rejection
carries no signal about whether any future approach could change it, and a run that correctly
diagnoses one of its own obligations as unachievable has no sound channel to say so. The result,
measured: v0.14.2 spent ~$50 and >30 iterations *after* every substantive check was green,
re-litigating a notification that could never be sent again; v0.14.1 burned an overnight run on
an environmentally impossible DoD item. Roughly 40% of recent spend is post-substance record
repair with an arithmetically certain outcome.

This wave builds the three mechanisms that fix the economics while keeping the judge exactly as
strict as it is:

- **A (#110)** — findings get structure and **severity** (`blocking | residual`); the verdict is
  computed from blocking findings only; the blocking/residual boundary comes from the PRD's own
  materiality rubric, defaulting to all-blocking when a contract supplies none.
- **B (#111)** — findings get a **curability bit**; an approach rejected only on incurable
  blocking findings takes a new edge — `rejected_incurable` — that **pauses the run and
  escalates to the operator** instead of replanning into certain rejection.
- **C (#112)** — **impossibility petitions**: the run may claim, once per obligation and with
  evidence, that an obligation is unachievable; *review* adjudicates the claim; a confirmed
  adjudication converts the obligation to a disclosed residual and is inherited across
  approaches.
- **D** — the reusable **review-economics contract section** (rubric, petition protocol,
  late-cure rule) lands in docs so every future PRD includes it by reference.
- **E** — version 0.15.0, v0.14.2's row settled, the roadmap, and one inherited erratum.
- **F** — closures with evidence, the full suite, the self-discharging final record, the
  closing report, the terminal summary.

   applies to you: use the petition protocol below rather than waiting for an amend that may
   come eight hours late.

## The contract-side prototype — this wave's own protection

### Materiality rubric (review: compute your verdict from blocking findings only)

**Blocking** — any of: a red test at the shipped sha; an unmet requirement letter (A–F below);
a false claim in a tracked artifact **that changes what a reader believes landed or its
result** (wrong commit attributed *for a change that shipped differently than described*, a
test result misstated, an issue state misreported, an invented notification); a missing
`sweep-sha` declaration; an issue closed without evidence; a secret exposed; a DoD predicate
below evaluating false.

**Residual** — everything else, including: imprecision or infelicity in prose; a stale snapshot
that is labelled as a snapshot; a missing cross-reference; formatting; an inaccuracy in a
record *about another record* whose correction would not change what a reader believes shipped;
a notification gap that has been cured under the late-cure rule. Residual findings are
recorded, must be listed in the closing report, and do not reject.

### Petition protocol

If you believe an obligation in this contract is unachievable, file a petition in
`artifacts/reports/v0.15-petitions.md`: one section per petition, stating the obligation (the
requirement letter or DoD item), a type (`environmental | temporal | contradictory-clauses |
external`), and the evidence. **Once per obligation.** Review must adjudicate every open
petition in its verdict: **CONFIRMED** converts that obligation to a disclosed residual for the
remainder of the run, all approaches included; **REFUTED** must state concretely what would
satisfy the obligation. Re-filing a refuted petition is a blocking finding.

### Late-cure rule

Any time-anchored duty in this contract (a send "the moment it happens", a record "at the
time") that is missed is **cured** by a late entry explicitly labelled late, plus a residual
line in the closing report. Once cured it is discharged and is not grounds for rejecting this
or any later approach. (This rule exists because its absence cost v0.14.2 two approaches.)

## What counts as done here

The disclosed-residual policy continues: a documentation gap, stale sentence, imprecise phrase,
missing cross-reference or cosmetic defect is disclosed in the closing report's residual list,
not repaired. Not covered: the six requirements, a red test, a blocking-false claim, an issue
closed without evidence. One honest attempt per task; deviations stated, never silent.

## Sequencing rule

No task's success criteria may require a state that a later task creates. Land enabling code
before anything that must be green because of it. If you find such a dependency mid-wave, split
the criterion honestly rather than deviating silently.

## Planning rules

Flat plan. No task names another in `dependsOn` unless it genuinely cannot start without that
task's output. Never let a docs, report, closure or notification task depend on a code task.
The closing report depends on nothing; it states what it observes.

# The requirements

## A — findings get structure and severity (#110)

Today review's whole verdict is `{"verdict": "verified"}` or
`{"verdict": "rejected", "reason", "findings_path"}` (`src/ralphd/prompts/review.md`), findings
free markdown. Build:

1. **A structured findings channel**: each finding carries at minimum `severity`
   (`blocking | residual`), `curable` (bool — requirement B consumes it), the PRD clause / DoD
   item it violates, and its evidence. Machine-readable (structured contract payload or a JSON
   sidecar next to `review-findings.md` — your design choice), human-readable markdown kept.
2. **Verdict from blocking findings only.** An approach whose findings are all residual is
   verified, with the residuals recorded and surfaced. Enforced engine-side, not merely asked
   of the prompt: the engine recomputes/validates the verdict against the findings' severities
   and refuses a contradiction through the normal contract re-ask ladder.
3. **The rubric comes from the contract.** Review reads the PRD's materiality rubric; the
   engine defaults to all-blocking when none is present. Pin the default with a test.
4. `review.md` updated to demand the structure; both peer gates (`plan_review`,
   `verdict_gate`) stay consistent with the new payload shape.

## B — incurable rejections pause instead of replanning (#111)

1. Review marks each finding `curable`: *would a fresh approach change this finding's truth
   value?* Temporal misses and environment facts are not curable; a fixable false claim is.
2. New review edge: when a rejection's blocking findings are **all incurable**, take
   `rejected_incurable` instead of the replan edge — the doomed-plan fault
   (`loop.py:4106-4203`) is the precedent for an edge that routes rather than starves. It:
   - **pauses the run** (existing pause machinery; pause credit already extends the deadline —
     proven in anger, `deadline_extended` reason `operator pause`),
   - **notifies the operator** with the findings and the exact `ralphctl amend` invocations
     that would resolve each one,
   - on resume after rulings, routes to **review** — the rulings are honoured against the
     existing work; a fresh approach is not planned. Make freshly-recorded rulings visible on
     this path (load them on resume, or document that a container restart is the mechanism —
     `state.apply_amendment_status_rulings` runs once per engine episode; do not silently
     leave the resumed episode unruled).
3. Any curable blocking finding present → replan exactly as today. Pin both directions with
   state-machine tests, and pin that `rejected_no_approach_left` still wins when approaches
   are exhausted.
4. Curability is asserted by review only. No run-side input reaches it except through C.

## C — impossibility petitions (#112)

1. A petition file format (documented in `docs/`): one petition per obligation, typed
   (`environmental | temporal | contradictory-clauses | external`), evidence required, once per
   obligation — a duplicate is a blocking finding.
2. Review adjudicates every open petition in its verdict: CONFIRMED → the obligation converts
   to a disclosed residual for the rest of the run; REFUTED → review states what would satisfy
   the obligation. The prompt demands it; the engine surfaces open petitions to review the way
   `_operator_amendment_review_context` surfaces rulings.
3. **Adjudications persist at run scope and survive `_archive_approach`** (`loop.py:6183`):
   approach N+1 inherits every CONFIRMED adjudication and must not re-litigate it. They do not
   need the `:ro` `/config` trust property — the hat that grants them is the hat that honours
   them — but they must be tamper-evident against the *worker* (e.g. review writes them;
   worker-phase edits to the adjudication record are a blocking finding).
4. Interaction, pinned by test: a CONFIRMED petition removes its obligation from the blocking
   set (A); if the remaining blocking findings are all incurable, B fires.

   ask, once, per obligation.

   advance: findings may be *classified*, never dropped; the all-blocking default when no

   never invent a send.

10. The closing report contains no blocking-false claim (per the rubric above).

6. Resume-after-rulings routes to review and the rulings are visible to it — pinned by test

7. A CONFIRMED petition converts its obligation to residual and survives approach archival; a
