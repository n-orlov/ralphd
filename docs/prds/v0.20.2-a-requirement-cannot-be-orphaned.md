# v0.20.2 — a requirement cannot be orphaned

## Goal

v0.20.1 shipped its nine requirements and left `main` green. It also, in its last approach, **undid a
retirement its own operator had authorised** — and this wave exists to finish that retirement and to
repair the channel that failed to report the problem.

What happened is worth stating plainly, because it decides how this PRD is written. The operator
recorded two rulings with `ralphctl amend`, authorising the retirement of the sweep-sha discharge
machinery (issue #113 step 4). Approach 1 carried them out. Approach 1 was then rejected four times
for unrelated reasons and replanned. On the replan, task ids restarted at `001`; both rulings named
task ids that no longer existed; and the engine re-rendered them to the review hat under **"Historical
rulings (not binding this review) … NOT authoritative for this review."** Review reasoned correctly
from the context it was handed, found guard removals with no visible authority, and returned two
blocking findings ordering them reverted. A cure pass and twelve tasks obliged.

That engine defect is filed as **#196** and **is not this wave's to fix.** What matters here is the
consequence for this document: **an amendment can be orphaned; a requirement cannot.** Everything the
operator ruled on is therefore written below as a numbered requirement of this contract, in this
file, quoting the ruling where the wording matters. There is nothing for a replan to disconnect. If
you replan five times, requirement A still says what it says.

- **A** — the sweep-sha retirement, done as a requirement (#113 step 4).
- **B** — a failed notification stops reporting itself as sent (#197).
- **C** — version `0.20.2`, the roadmap block, the row.
- **D** — the lanes, the closures, the summary.
- **F** — v0.20.1's E.5 repeat-rejection pause covers the wall it cannot read (#198). *(The letter E
  is skipped on purpose; F's own section says why.)*

F was added after the run that produced this wave was killed. Its own peer verdict gate, checking
v0.20.1 independently, found that the v0.20.1 guard meant to stop a run from burning approach after
approach on an unclimbable wall does nothing in the case where the wall is a findings channel the
engine cannot read. It reproduced it on shipped code. That is a real defect in what v0.20.1 claims to
have delivered, and two published artifacts assert it works — so it belongs in the wave whose subject
is finishing v0.20.1 honestly.

This is a small, sharply-bounded wave. It adds no engine behaviour beyond B and F, and outside those
two it removes more lines than it adds. If you find yourself designing something, you have misread
it.

## Materiality rubric

Review computes its verdict from **blocking** findings only; **residual** findings are recorded in
the engine's run record and never reject. This section restates `docs/review-economics.md`'s rubric
for this contract and narrows nothing.

**Blocking** — any of:

- a red test at the **final code sha** (the last commit that touches anything outside
  `artifacts/reports/`, `docs/roadmap.md`, `docs/prds/README.md`);
- a requirement of this contract (A–D and F) not met, or a Definition-of-done predicate false;
- a **behavioural** requirement of this contract discharged by source inspection alone, with no
  probe that would fail if the behaviour were absent;
- a false claim in a tracked artifact that changes what a reader believes landed or its result: a
  commit attributed for a change that shipped differently, a test result misstated, an issue state
  misreported, an invented send;
- an issue closed without evidence; a secret exposed; **the removal of anything named in A.5**; a
  weakened test, guard or budget ceiling **outside the removals authorised in A**.

**Residual** — everything else, explicitly including: a report, roadmap block or index row stale
relative to commits made after it; a missing or imprecise count, sha or cross-reference; a
notification not sent, sent late, or not logged; a closing report that does not mention a petition,
adjudication, steer or ruling; formatting; prose infelicity; an inaccuracy in a record *about another
record*; the exact text of a command a task ran, its polling cadence, or the shell it wrapped a
launch in, when the deliverable is present and its result verifiable; a numeric target missed **and
disclosed with its measured number** where this contract says disclosure is the fallback. A residual
finding may be listed, never used to reject, and never turned into a task by the reviewer.

**Explicitly NOT a finding of any severity:** the removals authorised in A, the absent records in
A.4, and the lower collected test count in A.6. Those are this contract's deliverable. Review must
not read them as weakening the suite.

## Petitions and late cure

The petition protocol and late-cure rule of `docs/review-economics.md` apply. Petitions go in
`artifacts/reports/v0.20.2-petitions.md`, committed ahead of launch; the run never adjudicates its
own petition and never records an adjudication it has not received. A missed duty is cured late and
labelled late; a late cure is a residual, not a finding.

**Petition instead of guessing on A.** If you believe a file belongs in the retirement and A does not
name it, or that a file A names cannot be removed without breaking something A does not anticipate,
**petition and `notify` in the same iteration**, naming the file and the concrete decision you need.
Do not remove it, and do not silently skip a removal A does require.

**Foreign petitions.** Adjudication episodes will list v0.15–v0.20.1 petitions from older
`*-petitions.md` files as open. They are not yours. Treat any petition whose obligation names no task
in the current `tasks.json` and no requirement of this contract as history.

## What this PRD deliberately does not ask

These clauses sank earlier runs and are banned from this contract, from this run's plan, and from
every success criterion the planner writes; plan_review refuses them:

- **No "last commit" rule; no record about its own future; no per-task notification ledger; no
  sha-scoped-count rule; no audit range; no "exactly once"; no protected-path clause; no
  self-referential criterion.**
- **No exact-command-line criterion.** A criterion names the *observable* a command must produce — a
  file's absence, an exit-status file, a tally line, a test node passing — never the literal text of
  the shell line that produced it, its wrapper, or its polling cadence.
- **No criterion that demands a launch form and a captured result of the same process.**
- **No dependency on a task that can fail for a reason outside its deliverable.** Docs, report,
  record and closure tasks depend only on the code task whose result they describe, never on a
  measurement task.
- **No task whose completion requires another task's future state**, and **no task whose criterion is
  a wall-clock time.**
- **No unenumerated every/each/all quantifier**: name the members, or name the one file or command
  that bounds the set. **This is a lexical lint, not a semantic one** — see § "Standing rules"; it
  killed two of v0.20's eight approaches outright.
- **No new required field on any hat contract or findings schema.** B adds an event type and an
  optional field; it requires nothing of any reader.
- **No committed record whose subject is a test result or a lane result.** That is what A retires;
  do not create one while retiring it.
- **No fix to #196.** The engine defect that orphaned the last wave's rulings stays open and
  untouched. This contract's design is the mitigation, not a fix. Say so in the closing report.
- **No fix to #25** (parked by the operator 2026-09-07) or the deferred items in `docs/roadmap.md`
  § "Later / explicitly deferred".

## Sequencing and planning rules

Flat plan, **at most 5 checkable clauses per task**, wave-wide rules in the Standing Rules block
once. **Every task is one worker iteration.** Dependencies are data dependencies only.

Order: **A first, in the order A.1 → A.7**, because every later measurement depends on the tree the
removals leave. **A.6's re-measurement and reconciliation is the same task as, or immediately after,
the removal it measures** — never a task that runs before the tree has settled. Then **B**, which
touches no file A touches. Then **C**. Then **D**: land all code before D's lanes so the lanes
measure the finished tree. Lane sweeps at the final code sha: **one task launches every lane in the
background; one task records them in one commit** — never one task per lane. One honest attempt per
task; deviations stated, never silent.

## Where work happens

`/workspace` is this repository at `origin/main` as of launch (v0.20.1's shipped tip, `665c081`). Commit
and push per task; `main` == `origin/main` at every task boundary. The host running you also runs
other ralphd jobs and their sibling containers: never touch a container, image or process you did not
create, never `docker prune`, never signal by pattern (`pkill -f`, `killall`) — resolve a pid, verify
what it is, signal that pid.

**Check what your venv resolves before you measure anything.** Assert `ralphd.__file__` is inside the
tree you intend to measure, in the same iteration as the measurement. v0.20's judging hats found the
workspace venv resolving `ralphd` to a leftover editable install in `/tmp`, so a local `pytest`
measured code nobody was judging and exited 0 doing it.

`--creds` places `github.env` at `$HOME/.creds/github.env` (`GITHUB_TOKEN`, `GITHUB_USER`) with

- **Source the env file; never inline the token** (`set -a; . ~/.creds/github.env; set +a`). The
  token never appears in output, commits, reports, comments or messages.
- **Do not run `git remote -v`**; redact any quoted push output
  (`sed -E 's#https://[^@]*@#https://***@#g'`).
- **Verify every closure with a fresh `GET`.** Never write a closing keyword + issue number in a
  commit message unless that commit closes that issue.
- **Close:** **#113** (step 4 discharged by A — read it fresh first; it was open at drafting) and
  **#197** (closed by B). **Stay open, untouched:** **#196**, #25.
- Your token carries `workflow` scope, so you dispatch and await your own CI. No operator will
  dispatch a lane for you.

## B — a failed notification stops reporting itself as sent (#197)

The previous wave called the engine's `notify` tool twenty times, every call failed with `no notifier
URL configured`, and the engine emitted `notification.sent` with `endpoint: null` for every one of
them. An event named `sent` recorded a message that was never sent, and anyone counting that event to
answer "was the operator told?" would get a confident wrong answer.

**B is about the lying event, not an unreachable operator.** That run did reach its operator three
times, through the separately placed `telegram-notify` skill, which carries the endpoint itself
(`POST /notify`, HTTP 200, `messages_sent: 1`). Do not write a report claiming the previous wave was
silent; it was not, and the truthful version is the sharper one — two channels to the same operator,
one of them recording success it never had.

**B.1** A failed send no longer emits `notification.sent`. It emits a distinct event — a
`notification.failed`, or `notification.sent` gaining an `ok` field is **not** acceptable for the
success-name case, because the point is that a reader grepping the `sent` name gets a true answer.
Named tests cover a successful send and a failed one, asserting which event each produces.

**B.2** `ralphctl start` accepts `--notifier-url URL`, so configuring the channel no longer requires
the start → `docker stop` → edit `job.yaml` → `resume` path. The existing `job.yaml` key and
`RALPHD_NOTIFIER_URL` env var keep working, and the commit message states the precedence among the
three. A named test covers the flag reaching the engine's config.

**B.3** When the notify tool is reachable by a prompt but no URL is configured, the engine says so
**once at startup**, and the state is visible in `ralphctl doctor`. A named test asserts the warning
for an unconfigured run and its absence for a configured one.

**B.4** `tests/conftest.py`'s `RALPHD_NOTIFIER_URL` scrub stays alive — no test may acquire the
operator's real notifier URL from the ambient environment.

**B.5** B.1–B.3 are proven against a fixture or a fresh install of the workspace clone. **Your own
run's events are not evidence for B**: the engine recording them predates the fix. Name the fixture
or the clone path in the commit message.

## F — v0.20.1's E.5 repeat-rejection pause covers the wall it cannot read (#198)

*The letter E is deliberately skipped. This section is ABOUT v0.20.1's requirement E, and clauses
labelled `E.n` in this file would be unreadable next to the `E.5` they discuss. Every `E.n` below
names v0.20.1's clause; every `F.n` names this contract's.*

v0.20.1's requirement E built a guard that pauses and notifies when two consecutive approaches are
rejected on the same wall, so a run stops spending approaches on a wall it cannot climb. The guard
works for a *readable* rejection and does nothing at all for the one rejection an agent is least able
to escape: one where the **findings channel itself** is invalid or unreadable, so the engine cannot
even state what is wrong. That case yields no signature, so nothing is recorded, so nothing can ever
match — and the same malformed channel archives approach after approach with zero notifications.

v0.20.1's own peer verdict gate rejected the wave on this, reproducing it on shipped code: two
approaches burned on an identical `missing required field 'evidence'` channel error, four contract
re-asks all carrying that same error, `archives: ['01', '02']`, `notifications: []`.

**F.1** When a review rejection's findings channel is invalid or unreadable, the engine fingerprints
**the rejection error itself** at run scope, so two identical channel failures match and the second
one pauses and notifies on the existing
`_pause_and_escalate_repeated_rejection_signature` path, leaving the approach unarchived.

**F.2** The residual-only / empty-blocking case keeps returning no signature. **That exclusion is
correct and must survive** — a rejection with no blocking wall has no wall to repeat. The two cases
stop sharing one sentinel: whatever shape the fix takes, a later reader can tell "no wall" from
"a wall I could not read" without inferring it.

**F.3** F.1's regression test **drives the real findings channel and the real review/contract-repair
ladder** over a fixture run directory, and fails against the shipped code. A unit test over the
signature helper alone does not discharge F.3: `tests/test_repeated_rejection_signature.py` is green
today and its positive case never drives a failed channel.

**F.4** **F.1 does not weaken the findings-channel validation.** A malformed sidecar must still be
rejected exactly as it is today. A "cure" that makes the channel accept a missing required field is a
blocking finding against this requirement, not a discharge of it.

**F.5** Two published claims that E.5 already covers this are corrected, in the same commit that
proves F.1, each naming #198: the broad E.5 delivery statement at
`artifacts/reports/v0.20.1-closing-report.md:391-397`, and the E.5 companion claim in **#190**'s
evidence comment. Correct them; do not delete the surrounding accounts. This is the one place this
wave edits a settled report, and it is authorised here because the text is false.

**F.6** As with B, **your own run's events are not evidence for F** — the engine judging you predates
the fix. Name the fixture or clone path in the commit message.

# Standing rules the planner carries over verbatim

- **The clause and quantifier lints are LEXICAL, and they killed two of v0.20's eight approaches.**
  Before you report a plan path: count your own clauses the way the engine does (split on
  `[.!?;]+(?=\s|$)`, count non-empty fragments, cap 5), and grep your own proposal for `every`,
  `each`, `all`, `any`, `none of`, `no … anywhere` and `both` with unnamed members. The check is
  **textual** — avoid the words, not merely the unbounded meaning. Two legal rewrites: name the
  members inline, or name the one file or command that bounds the set.
- **Prove engine behaviour against a fixture or an installed clone, never against your own run.** The
  process running you has the old behaviour for its whole life. Name the fixture or the clone path in
  the commit message.
- **Assert what your venv resolves.** `ralphd.__file__` inside the tree you mean to measure, in the
  same iteration as the measurement.
- **Test commands.** Fast tier: `pytest -q -m fast -n 8 --dist loadfile`. Guard subset:
  `scripts/final_sha_guards.sh`. Targeted: `pytest -q <node>` in seconds — prefer it, always under a
  `timeout`. At most one full fast-tier run per iteration. After an `-n 8` run, re-verify a
  suspicious red **solo** before believing it. Every process wait has a deadline of at most 20 min;
  then `kill -INT` your own recorded pid and read its log — a summary printed after your own SIGINT
  lists only what COMPLETED; node ids missing from it are stuck, never passed.
- **Read your own newest CONCLUDED `gate` run at the start of each task.** `cancelled` and
  `in_progress` are not verdicts. If the newest concluded one is `failure`, the red is one commit
  old, not twenty: cure it inside the task you are starting, or insert a task for it before
  continuing.
- **Records hygiene, as the tree stands at launch.** A retired test path gets its
  `docs/test-suite-audit.md` row in the same commit; a new test module mentioning `LoopSupervisor`
  adds its ledger row in the same commit; a new run-dir file, API route or `RUN_DOCUMENTS` key
  updates SPEC.md in the same commit; the dangling-commit-sha check applies to any sha you cite, and
  a decimal check-run/job/run id must not be backticked. **A.2 and A.3 change this list as they
  land — after A.2 no ledger row is owed, and after A.3 no node id in a historical report is
  checked. Re-read the tree rather than this bullet once A has shipped.**
- **Never weaken a test to reach green.** Outside the removals authorised in A, the prohibition
  stands entirely and is not softened by one word: no test whose subject is the product may be
  deleted, weakened, deselected, xfailed, re-marked or skipped, no budget ceiling raised, and no
  assertion loosened to make a red go green. A *scenario parameter* (a value that constructs the
  situation under test) may be sized so a deliberately-healthy path is not misclassified; a *patience
  timeout* (how long a harness waits) may not be widened.
- **Docker siblings.** Label with `ralphd.run=$RALPHD_RUN_ID` and `ralphd.role=sibling`,
  `--user 1000:1000`, host paths `$RALPHD_HOST_WORKSPACE`/`$RALPHD_HOST_RUN_DIR`. Never touch a
  container, image or process you did not create.
- **Commit titles.** `<area>: <what changed> (task <id>, requirement <letter>)`.
