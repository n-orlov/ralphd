# ralphd v0.7 — the loop you can trust unattended: containment, honest status, and a loop that learns

## Goal

v0.6 shipped a product whose surfaces mostly tell the truth about **numbers**.
This wave is about the two things that decide whether ralphd can be left running
overnight:

1. **Containment.** One bad iteration currently costs the whole run. A verifier
   killed its own supervisor and ended a 9h36m run (#48, #35). A slow iteration
   hits the 45-minute cap and takes the engine down with it (#28). An
   interruption of any kind rewrites a finished task's record as rejected and
   burns one of its three validation attempts (#45). A failed iteration
   silently eats the operator instruction it was carrying (#34), and the
   transcript that would explain why it failed is deleted by the resume (#44).
2. **Honest status after the fact.** v0.6 made a *running* run's numbers
   truthful. A run that died and was resumed still lies: liveness is read from
   a field the dead engine wrote (#31), `endedAt` and `reason` survive from a
   previous episode into a succeeded run's permanent record (#30), earned budget
   refunds are silently forfeited on resume (#32), a stale reflection tombstone
   is advertised beside the report that exists (#43), and `failed` means two
   different things so a task record cannot be read without a human explaining
   it (#33).

Third, and the reason this wave exists at all: **the loop must be able to
improve its own material.** v0.6's reflection produced a ready diff against four
prompts, and the only thing that turned it into work was an operator reading it
by hand. Eight of this wave's requirements are that diff (#35–#42), and one
(requirement P) is the verb that means the next wave does not need an operator
for this step.

The organising principle: **no single iteration may be able to end the run, and
no surface may report a previous episode's facts as this one's.** v0.6's
principle was "every number is either true or visibly unknown"; this wave extends
it across time — across a crash, a resume, and an approach boundary.

Every requirement below has evidence from a real run. Where something is a
judgement call rather than an observation, it says so.

## A. The reflection's prompt changes, reviewed, tightened and tested (#35–#42)

Each sub-requirement is its own issue, its own task and its own commit. The diff
is a starting point, not an authority — it was written by an agent reflecting on
its own run and has not been reviewed by anything.

**The common acceptance criterion, which the diff does not address at all:**

### A1. Sandbox safety in `task-verify.md` and `worker.md` (#35)

The highest-value item in the set: its absence ended a run. `task-verify.md`
currently has **no environment-safety rules at all** — its only hygiene rule is
"do not modify any files other than tasks.json", which says nothing about
processes or containers. Worse, its otherwise-correct instruction *"actually run
the commands, do not assume"* actively pushes a verifier toward destructive
experiments with no guard rails beside it.

Add: target processes by identity (a PID you spawned), **never** by pattern;
never signal, stop or remove a container, image or label you did not create; to
make a service unreachable prefer a shutdown endpoint, a closed socket or an
unroutable URL over killing anything; scratch work in `/tmp` or a `git worktree`,
never in the live workspace.

Pairs with **requirement I** (#48), which makes the hazard structurally
impossible. Neither substitutes for the other.

### A2. A terminally `failed` task has a deliberate resolution (#36)

`worker.md`'s completion signal recognises only `completed` and `skipped`, so one
`failed` task makes `<promise>COMPLETE</promise>` permanently unreachable, the run
never reaches `review`, and the stagnation guard replans the whole wave against
an already-finished repo. Say what to do: carve the residual gap into a **new
task** (the manoeuvre that rescued `043d` → `043e`), or relabel `skipped` with a
justification that explicitly does **not** claim the criteria were met, leaving
`validationAttempts` as the record.

Pairs with **requirement M** (#33), the vocabulary half.

### A3. Steering durability in the prompt (#37)

State the delivery-once property explicitly (fact 5) and require the worker to
persist the durable part of a steering note into the notes file **before** acting
on it, since the notes file is the one artifact every iteration reads. Worth
doing even after **requirement G** (#34) fixes the engine: it makes
multi-iteration operator instructions work by construction. This is not a
hypothesis — steering 008 opened with exactly this instruction and its two
numbered items were executed in two different iterations, which would otherwise
have been impossible.

### A4. Measure the suite once; budget it every iteration (#38)

`verify` burned **14.3h, 38% of the run's wall clock**, and iteration 115 chained
two full suites, hit the cap and killed the engine. Neither `worker.md` nor
`task-verify.md` acknowledges that iterations have a hard wall-clock cap or that

Two halves: **`planning.md`** measures the suite once (command, runtime, tiers,

This is the cheapest fix in the set relative to what it saves, and it is the one
most likely to pay for this wave's own cost.

# Phase 1 — containment: one bad iteration must not cost the run

Requirements B–I. **Do B first** — it is the root cause behind several of the
others' symptoms, and fixing it makes them observable rather than theoretical.

## B. The iteration timeout fails the iteration, not the engine (#28)

Fact 3 has the mechanism. The 45-minute cap currently **crashes the engine**: it
killed iteration 115 of `selfdev-v06-release` at 46 of 53 tasks and left a task's
edits uncommitted.

- Do not re-await a task `wait_for` has already cancelled. `result.timed_out` is
  already set; return the result and let the iteration fail normally.
- A timed-out iteration must reach the same recording path as any other failed
  iteration: `meta.json` written, `endedAt` set, the fault classified,
  budget/refund bookkeeping applied.
- Audit the file for the same shape elsewhere — `runner.py:159` and `:180` are
  the same idiom and must be checked, not assumed innocent.
- A `CancelledError` escaping an iteration must never be able to end the job.
  Since it is a `BaseException`, `loop.py:867`'s `except Exception` cannot see
  it; decide deliberately where the boundary belongs and say why in a comment.

**Tests.** An iteration that exceeds its timeout fails *that iteration* and the
engine keeps running and proceeds to the next one. A mutation case: reverting the
fix must make the test fail — a test that passes against the bug is worthless
here, and this bug's whole signature is that everything looks correct up to the
moment the process exits.

## C. No verdict means no verdict: an interrupted verify changes nothing (#45)

Fact 4 has the mechanism. **This is the defect that must land regardless of
everything else** — it silently rewrites the plan's record of finished, committed,
pushed work.

- Treat `interrupted` and `timed_out` exactly like `error_message`: no verdict
  rendered means `status`, `validationAttempts` and `validationNotes` are left
  byte-for-byte unchanged.
- The `validationNotes` currently written — "Verifier did not emit the
  task-verified sentinel." — is literally true and completely misleading. If a
  note is written on this path at all, it must distinguish "the verifier
  reached a negative verdict" from "the verifier never got to a verdict".

Reachable by ordinary means, not just by the exotic `pkill` that surfaced it:
`ralphctl abort`, `ralphctl stop`, a container stop, a host reboot, or — until B
lands — a merely slow verify.

**Tests.** Interrupt a verify iteration mid-flight; assert all three fields
unchanged. Same for a timeout. Include the mutation case that reverts the
condition to `error_message`-only and proves the test catches it.

## D. Fault classification: signals, and a bare `aborted` after traffic (#49)

Two shapes, one ladder (fact 8). Resolves **SPEC §16 open question 1**, which
this wave has the evidence to close.

- **A signal-killed iteration is not `faultClass: "work"`.** It was terminated
  before it could do anything wrong. This is not cosmetic: `work` is the class
  that reaches C's attempt-burning path, so this defect *feeds* that one.
- **A bare in-band `aborted` that ended well inside its own timeout, with no
  operator abort recorded, is infra even when traffic preceded it.** SPEC calls
  the duration discriminator "a threshold nobody can derive from first
  principles". `selfdev-v06-release` supplies one: iteration 145 got
  `error: aborted, faultClass: work` and died **39 seconds into a 45-minute
  cap**, as the leading edge of a DNS outage whose next five iterations were
  correctly classified `infra`, retried and refunded. Same outage, opposite
  treatment, decided only by whether a token had been emitted first — and 145
  was charged *and* destroyed steering note 007.
- **Keep the operator carve-out intact.** A genuinely operator-initiated abort
  must still never be `infra` (`faults.py:322-335`), or the retry wrapper fights
  the operator.
- Update `faults.py:347`'s comment, which points at the now-closed #23.

**Tests.** Table-driven over all four corners of (traffic / no traffic) ×
(operator abort recorded / not), plus the signal case, plus a duration boundary
case either side of whatever threshold you choose. Justify the threshold in a
comment; a magic number with no argument behind it is the thing SPEC objected to.

## E. A self-inflicted abort is not an operator abort (#46)

`LoopSupervisor.abort()` (`loop.py:215-228`) is the single path for "this run is
ending on someone's instruction" and writes the operator-termination marker
unconditionally, so a run shot from inside its own workspace records
`{"action": "abort", "reason": "signal 15", "source": "engine"}` — and no
operator did anything.

The marker is load-bearing, not cosmetic: `main.py:4262` uses it to guarantee a
run the operator killed is **never resurrected**. That guarantee is right and
must survive. But it currently also suppresses recovery for a run killed
accidentally from inside — which is exactly the case where resuming is correct.

- Give a self-inflicted termination its own class, distinct from an operator
  abort, and attach the evidence: the last tool call before the signal is already
  in `output.jsonl` and is the first thing an operator needs to read.
- `ralphctl status` must stop reporting the uninformative bare `reason: signal
  15` for this class.

**Ordering: E must land before O.** Flipping `auto_resume` on by default while a
self-inflicted death is recorded as an operator abort ships a recovery feature
that silently declines to act on the most common thing it should recover from.

**Tests.** The two classes are distinguishable in `status.json`; the
never-resurrect guarantee still holds for genuine operator aborts; a
self-inflicted abort is eligible for auto-resume. One test per class.

## F. No manufactured reflection failures, and no stale tombstones (#47, #43)

Two halves of one false claim, and fixing either alone still leaves a run that
can advertise a reflection failure it does not have.

- **#47:** once SIGTERM has been received and the child killer has fired,
  reflect cannot succeed. `_begin_reflect_retry_window` (`loop.py:1253-1288`)
  already reasons about this territory but governs **backoff**, not *whether to
  attempt* — so one dead-on-arrival attempt still runs and manufactures
  `reflection: failed (the reflect iteration was interrupted)`. Skip reflect
  when the engine is unwinding on a signal and record it as **not attempted**.
  `_reflect_failure` (`loop.py:1188-1215`) already has the notion that one shape
  is not a failure; this is a second.
- **#43:** `_record_reflect_outcome` writes `artifacts/reflection/FAILED.md` on
  failure and only *logs* on success (`loop.py:1230-1239`), so nothing removes a
  tombstone from an earlier episode. `selfdev-v06-release` finished
  `succeeded / verified` with both files on disk, and `FAILED.md`'s claim
  ("terminal state: aborted (verdict unverified)") is false in every particular.
  It is surfaced, not merely present: `reflect-failed` is a documented alias in
  `ARTIFACT_ALIASES` (`engine/state.py:1538-1545`), described as *"why the
  reflect phase left no report"*, so `ralphctl artifacts ls` offers it beside the
  report. Remove it when a reflect attempt succeeds.

Correctly scoped already, and do not regress it: `ralphctl status` gates its
reflection line on `reflect.get("ok") is not False` (`main.py:2265`), so the
defect is confined to the artifacts surface and the file on disk.

**Tests.** A SIGTERM'd engine records reflect as not attempted and writes no
`FAILED.md`. A fail → resume → succeed sequence leaves no `FAILED.md` and no
`reflect-failed` alias.

## G. Steering is consumed on success, not on delivery (#34)

Fact 5 has the mechanism. Steering note 007 was delivered into an iteration that
died 39 seconds in and was destroyed outright; the operator had to rewrite it.
Any failing iteration silently eats an operator instruction.

- Mark a note consumed once the iteration that carried it **completed**, not when
  it was handed over. An iteration that fails, is interrupted or times out leaves
  its notes pending for the next one.
- Do not let this double-deliver: a note must not be applied twice because the
  iteration succeeded but the marker write failed. Decide the ordering
  deliberately and comment it — at-least-once and at-most-once are both defensible
  here and the choice must be explicit.
- The `.consumed.json` marker's shape is on-disk state read by `GET /steering`
  and `ralphctl steer --list`; whatever you change must keep both rendering
  pending/applied correctly, live and from an on-disk snapshot.

**Tests.** A note delivered into an iteration that dies is still pending
afterwards and is applied by the following iteration. A note whose iteration
succeeds is consumed exactly once.

## H. A crashed iteration's transcript survives the resume (#44)

When an engine dies mid-iteration and the run is resumed, the resumed process
**reuses the same iteration number and directory**, overwriting the transcript of
the attempt that crashed. The trade is backwards in both directions: the crashed
attempt is the *more* interesting of the two, and the overwrite is silent.

This already cost v0.6's own reflection its primary evidence — iteration 115, the
45-minute timeout, is unrecoverable, and the finding had to be reconstructed from
events and side effects.

- Preserve the crashed attempt. Either keep the directory and let the resumed
  attempt take the next number, or archive the previous contents alongside — the
  engine already archives per-approach state under `approaches/NN/`, so the
  pattern exists.
- `max_iteration_number()` deliberately does not count an iteration dir with no
  `endedAt` (`engine/state.py:2618-2629`), and that reasoning is load-bearing for
  numbering. Whatever you change here must keep resume numbering monotonic — and
  note the interaction with **requirement L**, which changes what
  `iterations_used` is seeded from.
- Post-mortem is a shipped feature of this product, not a nicety. Destroying
  transcripts degrades it directly, and it compounds with C: the evidence for
  whether a burned validation attempt was fair is exactly what gets deleted.

**Tests.** Kill an engine mid-iteration, resume, and assert the pre-crash
`output.jsonl`, `prompt.md` and `meta.json` are all still readable.

## I. The agent cannot signal its own supervisor (#48)

`container/entrypoint.sh:25` ends with `exec ralphd-engine`, so **PID 1 has the
argv `ralphd-engine`**; `container/Dockerfile:44` sets `USER agent`, the same uid
iterations run as. `pkill -f ralphd-engine` from inside a task is therefore both a
match and permitted, and it ends the run. Resolves SPEC §15's deferred
**PID-namespace isolation** entry.

SPEC's own wording is the argument for doing the structural half rather than
relying on A1: *"it belongs here rather than in the 'an instruction is enough'
pile because an instruction demonstrably does not stop an agent from signalling
processes it should not."*

Two routes, and **the cheaper one is sufficient for this wave**:

- **Different uid** — run the engine as a uid distinct from `agent`.
  Structurally impossible to signal, costs the agent nothing it legitimately
  needs, and is a small change to the Dockerfile and entrypoint.
- **PID-namespace isolation** per iteration, as SPEC contemplates. The general
  fix; it also lets the engine distinguish "stop this one iteration" from "the
  container is being torn down", and closes the `docker`-commands-filtered-on-the-
  `ralphd.run`-label variant of the same hazard.

Take the uid route unless you find a reason it cannot work, and say in the commit
why. If PID-namespace isolation stays deferred, SPEC §15's entry must be rewritten
to say what *this* wave closed and what is left, not left standing unchanged.

**Watch for:** everything that currently assumes engine and agent share a uid —
file ownership under the run dir and the workspace, the config overlay under
`$HOME` (`config.py:31`), the editable install into user-site (fact 12), and the
`git` identity used for commits. This requirement is the most likely in the wave
to break something unrelated; give it its own task and a full-suite run.

**Tests.** `pkill -f ralphd-engine` from inside a task iteration does not end the
run. A docker-tier test, since it is a real container property.

# Phase 2 — honest status across a crash and a resume

Requirements J–N. Every one is a surface confidently reporting something false
about a *previous* episode.

## J. Liveness comes from docker, not from a field the dead engine wrote (#31)

Fact 7 has the mechanism and the four blind callers. An exited-but-present
container is invisible to `status`, `doctor`, `repair` **and** auto-resume, all
through one function.

- Distinguish three states, not two: **no container**, **container exists and is
  running**, **container exists and has exited**. The third is the one that
  currently has no representation.
- Fix it at `_dangling_run_entry`/`_container_running` so one change covers all
  four callers; do not patch the callers individually.
- An exited container with a run dir whose status says `running` is the
  definition of a dangling run — it must be reported as one, and it must be
  eligible for repair and auto-resume.

Not fixed by fixing #28: any engine death recreates it, and after B lands the
remaining deaths are the ones you most need to see.

**Tests.** With an exited-but-present container, all four surfaces report the run
as dangling. Assert on each of the four, not just on the shared helper — the
point of the bug is that the helper's return value was misread.

## K. `endedAt` and `reason` are episode-scoped (#30)

A resumed run keeps the previous episode's `endedAt` and `reason`. This is worse
than the mid-run cosmetic display the issue was filed for: it corrupts a
**succeeded** run's permanent record. `selfdev-v06-release` finished
`succeeded / verified` and `ralphctl status` reports `reason: signal 15` from a
`pkill` two episodes earlier. It is also observable right now on a *running* run
as an `ended` timestamp **before** its `started` timestamp.

- Clear or supersede both fields when an episode begins, in the same place
  `_run_job_core` already writes `health="ok"` and `infraWait=None`
  (`loop.py:1341-1346`).
- A terminal run's `endedAt`/`reason` must still be exactly what its **final**
  episode wrote. This is not "always clear it" — it is "never let a previous
  episode's value be read as this one's".
- Decide whether previous episodes' endings are worth keeping as history rather
  than discarding, and say why either way.

**Tests.** A resumed run never reports `ended` earlier than `started`. A run that
aborted, was resumed and succeeded reports no `reason` from the aborted episode.

## L. Refund counters survive a resume (#32)

Fact 6 has the mechanism. `selfdev-v06-release` forfeited **17 earned refunds**
and its official record says 145 iterations used where the guarantee implies 128.
The whole point of refunding an infra-faulted iteration is that an outage must
not cost the operator budget — a guarantee that silently expires at every resume
is not a guarantee.

- Persist the refund counters with the rest of run state, or seed
  `iterations_used` from a charged count rather than the raw
  `max_iteration_number()`. Either is defensible; pick one and say why.
- `iterationsUsed` in `status.json` is written as `n - self._infra_refunded`
  (`loop.py:837`) and is read by every surface that prints a budget. Whatever you
  change must keep a *live* run's number correct too, not only a resumed one.
- Note the interaction with **H**, which changes iteration-directory reuse and
  therefore what `max_iteration_number()` observes. These two requirements touch
  the same seed; do them in a deliberate order and test the combination.
- **This one has a permanent-record consequence you should note but not
  retroactively fix.** `docs/prds/README.md`'s v0.6 row reports 145 iterations
  because that is what the engine recorded. Do not rewrite the row: it faithfully
  reports what was written. Add a note where the tally is explained saying the
  figure is inflated by this defect, and reference the issue.

**Tests.** A run with refunded infra iterations, killed and resumed, still has
its refunds credited. Assert on `budget_left()` behaviour, not only on the
counter — the counter is the mechanism, the budget is the guarantee.

## M. `failed` says which kind of failed (#33)

`failed` currently means both "this requirement was not met" and "three
validation rounds were consumed", so a task record cannot be read without a human
explaining it — and, worse, a single `failed` task blocks a run from reaching a
verdict at all without an operator editing the record. That is not hypothetical:
it is precisely what happened to `043d`, cost roughly four iterations and two
steering notes, and was resolved only by an operator instructing a relabel.

- Disambiguate the two meanings. `skipped` already exists (fact 10), so this is
  about vocabulary and about what the loop does with each, not about adding a
  status.
- A run with a task in the exhausted-validation state must be able to reach a
  terminal verdict **without an operator editing the record**. That is the
  requirement; the status vocabulary is the means.
- Every tally that counts task statuses must stay correct, on every surface, for
  both meanings.
- Pairs with **A2**: even with better vocabulary the worker needs an instruction
  for the case, and even with the instruction the vocabulary must support it.

**Tests.** A run whose task exhausted its validation attempts reaches a terminal
verdict with no operator intervention. The counts on every surface distinguish
the two meanings. A migration case: a `tasks.json` written before this change
still reads correctly.

## N. A silently skipped verification gate warns (carried from #29)

#29 is fixed and closed (fact 9), but its "worth adding alongside" suggestion was
never implemented, and it is the part that actually cost the time: from outside,
an unbroken `worker` streak reads as a stall or a config change, not as a skipped
gate. The bug was silent for 17 iterations of a real run.

Emit a warning event the first time a worker iteration finds `pending_verify`
empty while completed tasks exist that this process never verified
(`loop.py:1432-1441`). Small, and it is the tripwire for every future variant of
this class.

**Tests.** A vigilant run whose verified-task record has been tampered with so
the gate computes empty emits the warning exactly once, not per iteration.

# Phase 3 — the two deferred decisions, taken

Requirements O and P. Both were operator decisions, both are now taken; the
reasoning belongs in SPEC, not only in a commit message.

## O. `auto_resume` defaults to ON (SPEC §15)

SPEC §15 states the intent to flip and names the single literal
(`AUTO_RESUME_DEFAULT`, `cli/main.py:381`). Its two stated preconditions — the
crash-loop guard, and "never resurrect a run the operator killed" — have
dedicated test modules and a great deal of real-run evidence behind them.

- Flip the literal. Fact 11 is the map of what that breaks and how to retarget
  it: one test whose name and docstring become false, one that fails by design
  because it asserts the roadmap still *promises* the flip. Retarget both in the
  way v0.6 retargeted `test_no_issue_was_closed_from_inside_the_run`; do not
  delete them.
- **Depends on E.** Do not land this before a self-inflicted abort is
  distinguishable from an operator abort, or the new default silently declines to
  recover the most common self-inflicted death.
- Move the entry out of SPEC §15 and into the shipped behaviour, and update
  `docs/roadmap.md`'s deferred section — which a test reads.
- An operator who wants the old behaviour needs a documented way to get it, and
  it must work per-run and registry-wide.

**Tests.** A plain `start` records auto-resume ON. Opting out works, per-run and
by default. The crash-loop guard and the operator-killed guard still hold with
the new default — those are the tests that matter, because the flip makes them
load-bearing for everybody rather than for opt-in users.

## P. A review-and-apply verb for the reflection diff (SPEC §16 Q6)

SPEC §16 Q6 asks whether the reflection diff should ever be applied
automatically, and observes that the loop's self-improvement "depend[s] entirely
on an operator reading a diff". This session was that operator: v0.6's
`suggestions.diff` was read by hand and turned into eight issues manually, and
Phase 0 of this PRD is the result. That is the gap, demonstrated, and it will
recur every wave.

Build the verb. Constraints, which are the whole design:

- **Nothing is applied without an operator's explicit act.** This is not
  auto-apply; SPEC's reasoning for keeping the loop out of its own prompts
  mid-flight stands, and requirement fact 2 is the reason a run could not benefit
  from it anyway.
- **Never mid-flight.** A run must not apply a diff to prompts it is executing
  under. Applying to the *workspace* is the operation; the next `start` picks it
  up via the image hash (fact 2).
- **Show before doing.** The operator sees the diff, and what it would touch,
  before anything is written. A verb that applies sight-unseen is the thing SPEC
  was avoiding.
- **Answer the conflict question SPEC names**, which is the reason this was a
  decision and not a task: what happens when two runs propose conflicting edits
  to the same prompt. Refusing cleanly with both diffs named is an acceptable
  answer; silently taking the second one is not.
- Partial application must be possible, or explicitly refused — a diff of eight
  suggestions where the operator wants six is the normal case, not the exotic
  one.
- CLI parity: this is a CLI verb. If the hub surfaces it, it surfaces the same
  capability, not more.

**Tests.** The verb applies a clean diff and the resulting image hash changes; it
refuses a diff that does not apply, and names why; it refuses to touch prompts of
a running run; the conflict case behaves as documented. A test that the operator
was shown the diff before it was written is worth having — this verb's whole
safety property is that nothing happens unseen.

# Phase 4 — close the loop

## Q. Close the issues, from inside the run

As v0.6 established, and with its lessons applied.

## Cross-cutting requirements

- **No silent behaviour change to a healthy run.** A run that never crashes,
  never resumes, never times out and never gets steered must behave exactly as it
  does today. The existing 153 test modules stay green without modification,
  except where a documented contract changed (`status.json` fields; the two
  auto-resume tests retargeted by O).
- **Nothing a single iteration does may end the run.** The containment property
  this wave is named for. If you find another way an iteration can kill its
  engine, file it and say so in the report even if you do not fix it.
- **A previous episode's facts are never reported as this one's.** The Phase 2
  property, and the general form of #30, #32 and #43.
- **Every fix carries a mutation case.** Half of this wave's defects are
  "everything looks right until the process exits" or "the field says something
  false". A test that passes against the bug proves nothing; the proof is that
  reverting the fix fails the test. This is not optional here.
- **Every new view and verb works with the container gone.** The on-disk snapshot
  path is not a degraded mode, it is how a finished run is read.
- **CLI parity is a hard rule.** The hub adds no capability the CLI lacks.
- Compressed schedules/timeouts must be settable so tests never sleep for real —
  B, F and G all involve timeouts and signals and must not add real sleeps to the
  suite.

- **No auto-application of the reflection diff** (P). A verb the operator drives,

- **No hot-swapping this run's prompts** (fact 2). Phase 0 lands committed
  changes for future runs.
- No retroactive rewriting of `docs/prds/README.md`'s v0.6 numbers (L). The row
  reports what the engine recorded; the note explains why that is wrong.
- No reopening #29 or re-litigating v0.6's closed issues. N is a new small
  requirement, not a reopening.
- **#24, #26 and #27 are out of scope** and stay open: the per-approach task list

  that drives an approach restart, which this wave does not build; the hub
  flicker is unrelated and small enough to bundle later.
- **#25 (composite steps / sub-jobs) stays open as a design question.** It needs
  a design conversation before it can be scoped, and it does not belong in a wave
  of contained fixes.
- No published image or wheel, no registry push, no PyPI. Still deferred (SPEC
  §15).
- No remote/daemon mode, no alternative agent runtimes, no parallel-job
  orchestration, no named env-wiring profiles. All still deferred, none

- No new `state` value.

## A note on size, for whoever plans this

This wave is **21 issues against v0.6's 9**, and v0.6 cost 55 tasks, 145
iterations and 332M tokens. Do not assume 2.3× — most of these are one-branch
engine fixes with a test, where v0.6's requirements included whole subsystems (a
cost model, an image lifecycle, four hub views). But do not assume they are free
either: I (the uid boundary) and P (the apply verb) are the two that can grow,
and M (`failed`'s vocabulary) touches every surface that counts tasks.

Requirement A4 exists partly to pay for this wave. v0.6 spent 38% of its wall
clock in `verify`; if the test-budget rules land early and are followed, that is
the single biggest lever on what this run costs — but note fact 2: A4's rules
land for the *next* run, not this one. Plan this run's own test budget by hand,
in the notes, on iteration one.
