## Materiality rubric

Review computes its verdict from **blocking** findings only; **residual** findings are recorded
in the engine's run record and never reject. This section restates `docs/review-economics.md`'s
rubric for this contract and narrows nothing.

**Blocking** — any of:

- a red test at the **final code sha** (the last commit that touches anything outside
  `artifacts/reports/`, `docs/roadmap.md`, `docs/prds/README.md`);
- a requirement of this contract (A–J) not met, or a Definition-of-done predicate false;
- a **behavioural** requirement of this contract discharged by source inspection alone, with no
  probe that would fail if the behaviour were absent (see G — this rubric line is the one v0.20
  did not have);
- a false claim in a tracked artifact that changes what a reader believes **landed or its
  result**: a commit attributed for a change that shipped differently, a test result misstated,
  an issue state misreported, an invented send;
- an issue closed without evidence; a secret exposed; a weakened test or guard; a test retired
  under B without the behaviour it pinned being covered by a named surviving test in
  `docs/test-suite-audit.md` § "Subsumed".

**Residual** — everything else, explicitly including: a report, roadmap block or index row that
is stale relative to commits made after it; a missing or imprecise count, sha or
cross-reference; a notification that was not sent, sent late, or not logged; a closing report
that does not mention a petition, adjudication, steer or ruling; formatting; prose infelicity; an
inaccuracy in a record *about another record*; the exact text of a command a task ran, its
polling cadence, or the shell it wrapped a launch in, when the deliverable the command was for is
present and its result verifiable; a numeric target missed **and disclosed with its measured
number** where this contract says disclosure is the fallback. A residual finding may be listed,
never used to reject, and never turned into a task by the reviewer.

## Petitions and late cure

The petition protocol and late-cure rule of `docs/review-economics.md` apply. Petitions go in
`artifacts/reports/v0.20.1-petitions.md`, committed ahead of launch; the run never adjudicates
its own petition and never records an adjudication it has not received. A missed duty is cured
late and labelled late; a late cure is a residual, not a finding.

**A petitioned task can deadlock you (#187, open).** Nothing triggers an adjudication episode
from the petition itself, and the engine will keep handing you worker iterations against a graph
it can prove is unselectable — v0.20 lost ~85 iterations and 3.6 h to this twice. Therefore: when
you petition, **`notify` in the same iteration**, naming the held task, every task that depends
on it, and the concrete decision you need. Then stop re-deriving the block. One notification got
the operator's release in 16 minutes; seventy identical correct diagnoses got nothing.

**Foreign petitions.** Adjudication episodes will list v0.15–v0.20 petitions from older
`*-petitions.md` files as open. They are not yours. Treat any petition whose obligation names no
task in the current `tasks.json` and no requirement of this contract as history.

## What this PRD deliberately does not ask

These clauses sank earlier runs and are banned from this contract, from this run's plan, and from
every success criterion the planner writes; plan_review refuses them:

- **No "last commit" rule; no record about its own future; no per-task notification ledger; no
  sha-scoped-count rule; no audit range; no "exactly once"; no protected-path clause; no
  self-referential criterion.**
- **No exact-command-line criterion.** A criterion names the *observable* a command must
  produce — a file, an exit-status file, a tally line, a test node passing — never the literal
  text of the shell line that produced it, its wrapper, or its polling cadence.
- **No criterion that demands a launch form and a captured result of the same process.**
- **No dependency on a task that can fail for a reason outside its deliverable.** Docs, report,
  record and closure tasks depend only on the code task whose result they describe, never on a
  measurement task.
- **No task whose completion requires another task's future state**, and **no task whose criterion
  is a wall-clock time.**
- **No unenumerated every/each/all quantifier**: name the members, or name the one file or command
  that bounds the set. **This is a lexical lint, not a semantic one** — see the Standing Rules
  block; it killed two of v0.20's eight approaches outright.
- **No new required field on any hat contract or findings schema** (see § "Read this" item 2).
- **No re-litigation of #185**, and no fix to #135, #187, #188, #189, #192, #179, #184, #183 or
  #181 — those are v0.21's, named here so you do not adopt them.

## A — the approach progress baseline survives a resume (v0.20 B.3, gate finding VG-001)

The gate's evidence, verbatim:

> `src/ralphd/engine/loop.py:6378` initializes `approach_start_head` on every invocation of
> `_step_worker`, rather than retaining the approach's original baseline. Lines 7802–7826 compare
> against that new baseline and archive/replan when it matches. The diagnostic
> `test_B3_progress_is_not_forgotten_after_pause_resume` uses the existing
> `_progressing_approach_supervisor` fixture: approach 1 of 3 commits real work, then correctly
> returns `('advance', 'paused', 'stagnation_last_approach')`. Its real pause/resume path returns
> to worker on approach 1. Three further quiet iterations then return `('advance', 'replan',
> 'stagnation_exhausted')`, and `approaches/01` exists. Thus the same progressing approach is
> actually archived after resumption.

Its `Cured when:`, verbatim and binding:

> At the replacement code sha, this commit → stagnation pause → resume → renewed stagnation
> sequence remains on approach 1, emits the pause/escalation, and creates no approach archive.
> The approach-wide progress evidence must also survive fresh-process resume and worker re-entry
> after steering. Pin these paths without weakening the unchanged silent-approach replan
> assertion.

**A.1** The approach's progress baseline is persisted in run state, not held in a `_step_worker`
local, and is read back on worker re-entry rather than re-derived.

**A.2** A named test drives commit → stagnation pause → **fresh-process** resume → renewed
stagnation and asserts the run stays on approach 1, emits the pause/escalation, and creates no
`approaches/NN` directory.

**A.3** A named test covers worker re-entry **after a steer** with the same assertion.

**A.4** The existing silent-approach replan assertion is unchanged — a genuinely stagnant
approach with no commits still replans. State in the commit message that you diffed it.

# Standing rules the planner carries over verbatim

- **The clause and quantifier lints are LEXICAL, and they killed two of v0.20's eight
  approaches.** Approach 5 died on an unenumerated quantifier, approach 7 on `successCriteria
  carries 6 checkable clauses` — three refusals on the *same* task, the offending clause shrinking
  to `(5) This task makes no commit` and still counted. Before you report a plan path: count your
  own clauses the way the engine does (split on `[.!?;]+(?=\s|$)`, count non-empty fragments,
  cap 5), and grep your own proposal for `every`, `each`, `all`, `any`, `none of`, `no … anywhere`
  and `both` with unnamed members. The check is **textual** — avoid the words, not merely the
  unbounded meaning. Two legal rewrites: name the members inline, or name the one file or command
  that bounds the set.
- **Test commands.** Fast tier: `pytest -q -m fast -n 8 --dist loadfile`. Guard subset:
  `scripts/final_sha_guards.sh`. Targeted: `pytest -q <node>` in seconds — prefer it, always under
  a `timeout`. At most one full fast-tier run per iteration. After an `-n 8` run, re-verify a
  suspicious red **solo** before believing it. Every process wait has a deadline of at most 20
  min; then `kill -INT` your own recorded pid and read its log — a summary printed after your own
  SIGINT lists only what COMPLETED; node ids missing from it are stuck, never passed.
- **Assert what your venv resolves.** `ralphd.__file__` inside the tree you mean to measure, in
  the same iteration as the measurement.
- **Records hygiene.** A commit that shifts line numbers in `state.py`, `loop.py` or
  `plan_ingestion.py` refreshes `artifacts/reports/conformance-ledger.md`'s citations in the same
  commit; a new test module mentioning `LoopSupervisor` adds its ledger row in the same commit; a
  retired test path or node gets its `docs/test-suite-audit.md` § "Subsumed" row naming a
  surviving node in the same commit; a new run-dir file, API route or `RUN_DOCUMENTS` key updates
  SPEC.md in the same commit.
- **Never weaken a test to reach green.** No deleted or loosened assertion, no raised budget
  ceiling, no weakened guard or prompt strictness — re-shape how a test drives the loop instead. A
  *scenario parameter* (a value that constructs the situation under test) may be sized so a
  deliberately-healthy path is not misclassified; a *patience timeout* (how long a harness waits)
  may not be widened. Keep `tests/conftest.py`'s `RALPHD_NOTIFIER_URL` scrub alive.
- **Docker siblings.** Label with `ralphd.run=$RALPHD_RUN_ID` and `ralphd.role=sibling`,
  `--user 1000:1000`, host paths `$RALPHD_HOST_WORKSPACE`/`$RALPHD_HOST_RUN_DIR`. Never touch a
  container, image or process you did not create.
- **Commit titles.** `<area>: <what changed> (task <id>, requirement <letter>)`.
