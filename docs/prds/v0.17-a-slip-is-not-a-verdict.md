# v0.17 — a slip is not a verdict

## Goal

v0.16 shipped mid-run petition adjudication and closed #114; the operator then took the loop
apart end to end — every hat, every contract, every guard, every test tier — and filed the
findings as issues #119–#130, re-verified citation by citation at `6bd1231`, alongside the
pre-existing #117, #115 and #116, and the host-side #118. The headline is an enforcement
gradient that runs backwards: the worker, whose contract mistakes are the cheapest in the
system, is defended by the re-ask ladder at five sites; the three *judging* hats — verify,
review, verdict_gate — are defended at zero, one narrow, and zero sites, and their safe
defaults are the most expensive events a run can have. One malformed final line from verify
burns 1 of 3 validation attempts; from review it archives the approach with `reason: None`;
from verdict_gate it throws away a review-VERIFIED approach. A formatting slip is not a
judgement, and this wave stops charging it as one.

This wave (#119–#130, #117, #115's lint half, #116, #118):

- **A (#119, #120, #121)** — **the judges get the ladder**: the existing uncharged re-ask
  machinery is wired at verify, review and verdict_gate; verify's prompt stops promising an
  injection the harness never sends; the one untested contract cross-check in the system gets
  its tests.
- **B (#122, #128)** — **a claim means something**: a steer that honestly reports
  `settled: false` gets the message redelivered (bounded); a worker whose repaired reply says
  `complete` gets the completion applied instead of discarded into the stagnation counter.
- **C (#123, #124, #127, #129)** — **legal paths and honest bookkeeping**: `failed → skipped`
  becomes a declared, contract-carried move instead of an instruction privsep forbids;
  plan-ingestion exhaustion writes blocker signatures so identical walls end the run instead
  of eating the approach budget; the scheduler's resume buckets honor `priority`; the dead
  `return []` goes.
- **D (#117, #125)** — **the second judge becomes real**: the router's question carries
  evidence, plan_review's undecidable branch gets a declared deterministic policy, both peer
  gates get `ralphctl start` flags and `effective()` visibility, verdict_gate gets its own
  findings path with authorship in the composite PRD, and a peek past its blindness is
  detected and recorded.
- **E (#115 lint half, #116)** — **the plan-time criteria bound**: a sixth ingestion lint for
  unenumerated quantifiers and clause-count overruns, the seven named evasion fixtures, and
  the engine-side refusal of worker/planner-authored criteria rewrites (task 008's residual).
- **F (#126, #118)** — **the loop sees itself, the host stays clean**: reflect's
  self-improvement scope covers all eight hat prompts; job containers stop dumping
  `sleep` cores onto the host.
- **G (#130)** — **the coverage debt**: black-box e2e for both peer gates, the steer tool
  spool at the two-uid tier, the gate-twice-in-one-run sequence, and the rest of the audit's

- **I** — the closure of all sixteen issues with evidence, the full suite, the sweeps, the

## Read this before anything else

1. **Your engine is 0.16.0 — everything you are fixing is live in the loop that runs you.**
   Your own verify, review and verdict_gate have **no re-ask ladder**: one malformed final
   line from your own judging iterations costs a validation attempt or an approach, exactly
   as §A describes. Be exact with final-line contracts — last non-blank line, one JSON
   object, nothing after it. The petition machinery (#114) is live and protects you: file
   petitions through the documented format rather than burning attempts, and trust the
   mid-run adjudication.
2. **This run is the first ever launched with `peer_plan_review` and `peer_verdict_gate` on**
   (plus `vigilant`). Your plans will face a peer reviewer before the first worker pass
   (2 revisions per approach, fail-open), and your approach-end VERIFIED will face a blind
   second judge whose rejection costs a fresh approach. Both gates fail open or fail safe by
   design; neither has ever run outside the test suite (#117, #130). If a gate misbehaves in
   a way the issues predict, that is *evidence* — record it in the notes and the closing
   report; do not fight it, and do not claim your own run as test coverage for what you
   build. Tests are the whole of the evidence for this wave's own deliverables.
3. **The judge stays strict — that is a design constraint.** Review has been factually right
   in every rejection across six waves. Nothing here weakens a check: the re-ask ladder
   changes what happens *before* the safe default, never the default itself, and the
   deterministic plan_review policy replaces a blind coin flip, not a judgement.
4. **The operator may be asleep for your entire run.** Petition rather than wait for an
   amend. Every time-anchored duty is governed by the late-cure rule.

## The contract — rubric, petitions, late cure

The materiality rubric, petition protocol and late-cure rule of `docs/review-economics.md`
apply to this contract in full (this PRD is that document's third instance by reference).
Petitions go in `artifacts/reports/v0.17-petitions.md`. The three v0.16 contract rules bind
this run's planner and validator unchanged:

1. **The scratch-state rule.** No success criterion, validation demand, or review finding may
   condition on the content or format of untracked run state beyond the existence of an
   artifact a criterion explicitly declares. A pointer to a declared artifact is sufficient
   evidence of its content.
2. **Sha-scoped figures.** Every count, tally, or numeric claim written into a tracked record
   names the commit it was computed at ("at `<sha>`: N").
3. **The stranding taxonomy is banned from this wave's own plan**, and so is the criteria
   maximalism #115 names: no unenumerated universal quantifier, at most ~5 checkable clauses
   per task, wave-wide obligations in the Standing Rules block once — never copied per-task.
   Your own 0.16.0 planning/verify/plan_review prompts already carry these rules (v0.16
   task 035); follow them, and remember your validator verdicts on substance and discloses
   tails.

## What counts as done here

The disclosed-residual policy continues: documentation gaps, stale-but-labelled snapshots,
imprecision, and cosmetic defects are disclosed in the closing report's residual list, not
repaired. Not covered: the nine requirements, a red test, a blocking-false claim, an issue
closed without evidence. One honest attempt per task; deviations stated, never silent.

## Sequencing rule

No task's success criteria may require a state that a later task creates. Land enabling code
before anything that must be green because of it. If you find such a dependency mid-wave,
split the criterion honestly rather than deviating silently.

## Planning rules

Flat plan. No task names another in `dependsOn` unless it genuinely cannot start without that
task's output. Never let a docs, report, closure or notification task depend on a code task.
The closing report depends on nothing; it states what it observes.

## Where work happens

`/workspace` is a checkout of this repository at `origin/main` as of launch. Commit and push
per task; `main` == `origin/main` at every task boundary.

## Pushing, and closing issues from inside the run

`--creds` places `github.env` at `$HOME/.creds/github.env` (`GITHUB_TOKEN`, `GITHUB_USER`)
with `gitconfig` and `git-credentials`; push and the REST API work with no setup.

- **Source the env file; never inline the token** (`set -a; . ~/.creds/github.env; set +a`).
  The token never appears in output, commits, reports, comments or messages.
- **Do not run `git remote -v`**; redact any quoted push output
  (`sed -E 's#https://[^@]*@#https://***@#g'`).
- **Verify every closure with a fresh `GET`.** Never write a closing keyword + issue number in
  a commit message unless that commit closes that issue.

# The requirements

## A — the judges get the ladder (#119, #120, #121)

The re-ask ladder (`contract_enforcement.enforce_contract` + the uncharged
`_ask_contract_fix`, `loop.py:3998` — fenced-block rescue first at zero model calls, then a
grounded re-ask on the hat's own model, uncounted, under `byPhase.contract`) is wired for
planning, worker (five sites), the generic dispatcher, reflect, and one narrow review channel.
It is absent exactly where a slip is priciest. Wire it, changing **no** safe default — the
default becomes what happens *after* the re-asks are spent, never instead of them:

1. **verify** (`_verify_task`, `loop.py:7147`, inline parse at `7205-7221`): wrap the parse in
   `enforce_contract` with `extra_validate` = `transitions.verify_report_errors` plus the
   `task_id == tid` cross-check. A validation attempt is consumed only on ladder exhaustion.
2. **review**: widen `_review_contract_ladder`'s entry condition (`loop.py:5550`) to also
   admit "no parseable payload" and "schema-invalid payload". It already re-reads the
   findings channel each attempt; nothing else moves. The engine-side verdict recomputation
   from blocking findings is untouched.
3. **verdict_gate**: the same shape, pushed inside `_verdict_gate_verdict` (`loop.py:4489`) so
   the ordinary site (`loop.py:4466`) and the off-budget grace site (`loop.py:6531`) inherit
   it from the one parser.
4. **verify's prompt stops lying about its context (#121)**: `_verify_task`'s `extra`
   (`loop.py:7186-7191`) gains
   `- **validationAttempts**: {n} of {VALIDATION_ATTEMPT_LIMIT}` with an explicit
   `THIS IS THE FINAL ATTEMPT` marker on the last one (the value is already in hand at
   `loop.py:7182`); `task-verify.md`'s step-4 wording matches the injected field names
   verbatim, pinned lexically — the last-attempt handoff (and therefore the exhaustion
   petition's evidence, #114) stops firing on luck.
5. **the `task_id`-mismatch guard gets its tests (#120)**: at the in-process
   `LoopSupervisor` tier — task `001` under verification, verifier answers
   `{"task_id": "002", "verdict": "verified"}` → after the ladder (A.1 lands first) is
   exhausted, task `001` goes `validation-failed` with the mismatch text in
   `validationNotes`, task `002` is byte-identical, and a `contract.violation` event carries
   the guard's message; a negative twin with the correct id verifies normally.

Acceptance, per #119: a prose final line from verify, repaired on re-ask, leaves
`validationAttempts` untouched and the task reaching `validated`; from review, it does not
archive the approach or write a composite PRD; from verdict_gate, it does not spend an
approach. Ladder **exhaustion** at each site keeps today's safe default byte-identical
(existing tests pass unmodified) and still never solely ends a run with budget remaining.
Re-asks at all three sites are uncounted, under `byPhase.contract`.

## B — a claim means something (#122, #128)

1. **Steer `settled: false` redelivers, bounded (#122, option 1).** Consumption today keys
   only on `fault_class is None` (`loop.py:1685-1687`), so an honest "couldn't finish" is
   consumed and never redelivered. Instead: a clean steer iteration whose payload says
   `settled: false` leaves the message deliverable; a per-message redelivery counter
   (`steerRedeliveries[<name>]` in status.json, mirroring `planReviewRevisions`, surviving
   resume) bounds the loop; on the cap the message is marked settled-by-exhaustion in the
   ledger with the reason. `settled: true` consumes exactly once as today (existing
   consume-on-success and dying-iteration-redelivery tests unchanged). `ralphctl` and
   `GET /steering` show a not-settled message as outstanding, not applied.
2. **A repaired worker verdict is applied, once (#128).** After `enforcement.ok` at the
   generic schema site (`loop.py:5041-5090`), re-enter the verdict dispatch exactly once with
   the repaired payload, so a repaired `complete` / `unsatisfiable` / `plan_complete` is
   applied by the branch that owns it — each of those branches keeps its own cross-checks.
   A repaired-and-applied `complete` no longer reads as a no-progress iteration to the
   3-strike stagnation breaker (`loop.py:5275-5296`). Ladder exhaustion at this site still
   falls through to ordinary stagnation bookkeeping (existing site-5 tests unmodified); no
   second retry loop — the re-entry happens at most once per iteration, pinned by test.
